⚠️ Critical Red-Flag Symptoms (Do NOT Wait)

🫀

Acute Chest Pain / Pressure

Crushing retrosternal pain radiating to left arm, neck, or jaw accompanied by diaphoresis, shortness of breath, and nausea.

Immediate 108 Cardiac Call
🧠

Stroke Symptoms (FAST)

Sudden facial droop, arm weakness or numbness, slurred speech, confusion, or sudden severe headache ("worst headache of life").

Time-Critical Reperfusion Window
🫁

Severe Respiratory Distress

Inability to speak in full sentences, cyanosis (blue lips/fingers), severe stridor, choking, or SpO2 dropping below 90%.

Immediate Oxygenation & ICU
🩸

Heavy Arterial Bleeding

Pulsating or uncontrollable bright red bleeding from trauma, deep lacerations, or major compound fractures.

Apply Direct Pressure & Tourniquet

🩺 Critical First-Aid Protocols (While Waiting for Ambulance)

🫀 CPR Protocol (Unresponsive Patient)

  • 1 Check responsiveness & breathing (10 seconds max).
  • 2 Call 108 immediately and put phone on speaker.
  • 3 Place hands in center of chest, push hard and fast (100–120 beats/min).
  • 4 Continue continuous chest compressions until medical personnel arrive.

🩸 Severe Bleeding Control

  • 1 Apply firm, direct continuous pressure with sterile gauze or clean cloth.
  • 2 Elevate the bleeding limb above the level of the heart if possible.
  • 3 Do NOT remove soaked dressings; layer fresh cloth on top.
  • 4 If severe arterial spurting persists, apply a tourniquet 2 inches above wound.

🧠 FAST Stroke First-Aid

  • 1 Face: Ask person to smile. Does one side of the face droop?
  • 2 Arms: Ask person to raise both arms. Does one arm drift downward?
  • 3 Speech: Ask person to repeat a simple phrase. Is speech slurred?
  • 4 Time: Note the exact time symptoms began and call 108 immediately.

🔥 Burns & Scalds

  • 1 Immediately cool the burn with gentle cool running water for 15–20 mins.
  • 2 Remove constrictive jewelry/clothing before swelling begins.
  • 3 Cover burn loosely with clean cling wrap or non-adherent dressing.
  • 4 NEVER apply ice, butter, toothpaste, or burst any blister.
